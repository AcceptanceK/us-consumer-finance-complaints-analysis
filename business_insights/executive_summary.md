# Executive Summary

*US Consumer Finance Complaints & Service Performance | January–March 2025*

## Overall Complaint Trends

The analysis covers 50,430 CFPB complaints: 57% concerning checking or savings accounts and 43% concerning credit cards.

January accounted for 27,440 complaints (54% of the quarter), compared with approximately 11,700 in February and 11,300 in March. Checking or savings complaints declined by 70.3% in February, consistent with an unusual January spike subsiding rather than necessarily indicating improved service.

Additional investigation identified a concentration of January's checking or savings complaints at Navy Federal Credit Union and Capital One, with overdraft-related complaints increasing around January 15.

From February onward, credit cards generated more complaints each month than checking or savings accounts.

## Key Findings

Untimely responses were relatively uncommon. Among companies with at least 30 complaints, eight recorded untimely responses.

Block recorded the highest number (31; 2.6%), while Robinhood had the highest untimely response rate (10.7%). Bank of America recorded a rate of 0.4% across 2,498 complaints.

Block's checking or savings complaints in Florida and Georgia warrant attention, with untimely response rates of 7.1% and 5.3%, respectively.

Credit card complaints about incorrect information on credit reports increased by 42.5% from February to March. Meanwhile, account management and purchase disputes remained consistently high-volume issues.

## Strategic Recommendations

Financial institutions should monitor complaint volumes daily by company, product and issue, using alerts to identify unusual increases.

Management should investigate Block's complaint-handling delays in Florida and Georgia, review Robinhood's response processes, and examine the increase in credit-reporting complaints.

Persistent high-volume issues should have clear operational owners and regular root-cause reviews.

## Data Limitations

The three-month period limits conclusions about long-term trends.

Untimely response counts are small at the state-product level, and company complaint volumes cannot be fairly compared without customer population data.

The CFPB timeliness indicator measures whether responses met reporting deadlines, not resolution quality. Complaints without known timeliness status were excluded from rate calculations.
