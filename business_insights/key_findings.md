# Key Business Findings

## Finding 1: Banking Product Complaint Trends

**Business insight**

Between January and March 2025, the CFPB recorded 50,430 complaints concerning checking or savings accounts and credit cards.

Checking or savings accounts generated the most complaints (28,889), compared with 21,541 for credit cards.

January accounted for 27,440 complaints, approximately 54% of the quarterly total. Checking or savings complaints fell by 70.3% in February and a further 7.0% in March.

Credit card complaints declined by 31.5% in February before increasing slightly by 0.7% in March.

Further investigation of the complaint records indicated that January's checking or savings spike was concentrated in Navy Federal Credit Union and Capital One, with a substantial number of overdraft-related complaints beginning around January 15.

The data does not establish what caused this increase.

**Strategic recommendation**

Financial institutions should monitor complaint volumes daily by company, product and issue to identify unusual increases early.

Management should investigate the January spike, including possible policy changes, operational disruptions and customer communication problems.

Institutions experiencing sudden increases should investigate the underlying causes, communicate with affected customers and adjust complaint-handling capacity where necessary.

Longer historical data is needed to establish normal complaint volumes and evaluate whether service performance is improving.


## Finding 2: Company Response Timeliness

**Business insight**

Untimely complaint responses were relatively uncommon among the financial institutions analysed.

Block recorded the highest number of untimely responses (31 of 1,207 complaints), representing a 2.6% untimely response rate.

Robinhood had the highest untimely response rate at 10.7% (19 of 177 complaints), followed by Optum Bank at 4.4% (3 of 68).

Bank of America received 2,498 complaints but recorded only nine untimely responses (0.4%). Capital One and Citibank recorded no untimely responses despite receiving substantial complaint volumes.

Block and Robinhood accounted for 50 of the 71 untimely responses among the 20 companies displayed.

These findings demonstrate why complaint volume and response timeliness should be measured separately. However, timely responses do not necessarily indicate satisfactory complaint resolution.

**Strategic recommendation**

Robinhood should prioritise investigating its relatively high untimely response rate, while Block should examine the operational factors contributing to its larger number of untimely cases.

Management should investigate complaint complexity, staffing capacity, response delays and possible backlogs.

Companies with strong recorded timeliness performance could be examined for useful complaint-handling practices, without assuming that their overall customer service is superior.

Additional historical data, complaint outcomes and information about company size would help management make more reliable performance comparisons.


## Finding 3: Emerging and Persistent Complaint Issues

**Business insight**

Credit card complaints involving incorrect information on credit reports recorded the largest February-to-March increase, rising by 156 complaints (42.5%), from 367 to 523.

However, January recorded 1,242 complaints in this category, suggesting that March's increase may represent a rebound rather than a sustained upward trend.

Managing a checking or savings account was the largest recurring complaint issue, generating 13,839 complaints during the quarter. Despite January's unusually high volume, complaints remained substantial in February (2,769) and March (2,838).

Credit card purchase disputes were another persistent concern, generating 4,124 complaints across the three months. Complaints about obtaining credit cards and closing checking or savings accounts also remained consistently high.

These findings show that rapidly growing issues and consistently high-volume issues both require management attention.

**Strategic recommendation**

Financial institutions should investigate the increase in credit-reporting complaints, focusing on the companies involved, the nature of reported errors and the effectiveness of correction processes.

For recurring high-volume issues, management should identify underlying operational weaknesses, assign clear accountability and review customer communication and dispute-handling processes.

Complaint monitoring should measure both absolute volume and percentage growth, with unusual spikes investigated separately.

Further analysis of complaint sub-issues, company-level patterns, outcomes and longer historical periods would help identify the most appropriate corrective actions.


## Finding 4: Geographic and Product-Level Complaint Prioritisation

**Business insight**

Block's checking or savings complaints in Florida and Georgia emerged as the highest-priority combinations for investigation.

Florida recorded five untimely responses from 70 complaints (7.1%), while Georgia recorded four from 76 (5.3%). Both rates exceeded Block's overall untimely response rate of 2.6%.

Together, these states represented approximately 12% of Block's complaints but accounted for 29% of its untimely responses.

Block also appeared across multiple other states with recorded untimely responses, suggesting that management should investigate both local patterns and broader complaint-handling processes.

Robinhood's California checking or savings complaints recorded a 9.5% untimely response rate, although the combination contained only 21 complaints.

By comparison, Bank of America's California checking or savings combination generated 319 complaints but only two untimely responses (0.6%).

These differences demonstrate that complaint volume, response rate and geographic concentration must be considered together when prioritising investigations.

**Strategic recommendation**

Block should investigate its Florida and Georgia complaint-handling processes first, examining the reasons for delays, complaint types and whether untimely responses occurred during particular periods.

Management should also review Block's wider operations to determine whether similar problems affect multiple states.

Robinhood's California complaints should be monitored for recurring delays, while institutions with high complaint volumes and strong timeliness performance may provide useful operational benchmarks.

Financial institutions should introduce monitoring that considers both untimely response volumes and rates, with appropriate minimum complaint thresholds.

Further analysis should incorporate complaint severity, response delays, resolution outcomes, customer population by state and longer historical periods.

Because some combinations contain very few untimely responses, these findings should guide investigation rather than establish definitive comparisons of service quality.
